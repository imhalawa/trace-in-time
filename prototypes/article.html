What building reliable systems taught me about trust

A few thoughts on the small choices that make things easier to depend on.

Reliability has a way of revealing itself in the moments when something goes wrong. Not in whether a system can avoid every mistake, but in how clearly it tells us what happened and what can be done next.

I’ve come to think of trust as something built through small, repeated choices: making behaviour understandable, leaving a useful trail, and creating space for people to recover when things don’t go to plan.

Start with the question

Before changing a system, I try to understand the problem it is really being asked to solve. Sometimes the most useful work is simply finding a better question.

A system earns trust when the people using it can understand what it is doing, especially when the answer is uncomfortable.

That applies to the people maintaining a system too. A clear signal in a difficult moment can be more valuable than a perfect-looking dashboard when everything is calm.

Leave a trail

When a decision has consequences, it helps to make those consequences visible. A useful record tells the next person what happened without asking them to reconstruct the whole story.

⌘ YAML
event: payment.updated
status: awaiting_confirmation
next_step: check_with_provider

This is an illustrative example, but the principle is simple: make the next step easier to see.

Trust is built over time

There is no single feature that makes something trustworthy. It emerges from many decisions made consistently, especially the ones that only become visible when a person needs help.
