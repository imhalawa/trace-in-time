A Trace in Time

25 Sept 2026·3 min read·updated 25 Sept 2026

Capture as many moments as you can. Laugh while you have the chance. Try the things you want to experience. Don’t be harsh on yourself, and more importantly, don’t rush anything.

Don’t rush a meal, a dream, or a relationship with someone you like. Or try. You’ll be surprised, and you’ll learn the lesson the hard way.

Some lessons take longer to understand than they take to happen. You move forward, thinking you’ve left them behind, only to recognize them later in your choices, your expectations, and the things you still hesitate to try.

Looking through old photographs reminded me how much time had passed. In my mind, some of those moments still felt like yesterday.

It was like that moment in Interstellar, trying to tell your past self: Don’t. Please.

The choices I had made. The routes I had taken. The people I had hurt without meaning to, because I wasn’t paying attention. The limits I had set for myself because somewhere along the way, I had accepted the belief that certain things were not for me.

The experiences I avoided because I feared judgment. The possibilities I dismissed before giving myself a chance. How much of that was what I wanted, and how much was what I had learned to believe?

With time, I’ve started understanding myself better. Things that once felt tangled are becoming clearer. I can appreciate the people I’ve met and what I’ve learned without letting their beliefs decide how I live.

Part of me still struggles with how easily paths can separate. Someone can mean a great deal to you and still not remain in your life. Don’t complicate it, I tell myself. Not everything needs to become an endless question about what I should have done differently.

Understanding that doesn’t change the past. But it changes what I carry forward.

I’ve also learned to be more thoughtful about what I share and with whom. Some things belong with people I trust. Sometimes an honest conversation happens with someone I barely know. People passing through, a small span in each other’s timelines. Not every meaningful encounter has to become a permanent part of your life.

Maybe that’s why I keep coming back to time and how things change within it. Life talks in waves. Things are never as separate as they seem. An experience doesn’t stop where you think it ends. It carries into what you believe, what you accept, and what you do next.

You might have noticed it yourself: the same situation, over and over and over, like an endless loop. Sometimes the pattern is in what we believe and how those beliefs come together to shape other beliefs. Thoughts become part of the world through the choices we make and the routes we allow ourselves to take.

Composition is a key here. It starts with an idea, as simple as it can be. It mixes with another, or let’s say it’s composed with another, to shape something else. That becomes part of the next composition, in a recursive, endless loop.

It’s an amazing architecture of being, at an abstract level. We can reduce it down to primitives and compositions. Small parts, and what they become together.

And when I think about changing those patterns, I have to remind myself that change needs time. It doesn’t happen in separate pieces, one neatly after another. Remember, it’s a wave.

That is the picture I have in mind. Not only a fixed physical shape, but behavior unfolding over time. What we do, what we repeat, and what we gradually learn to do differently.

Change is the only constant from birth toward death. You’re here for a reason, not necessarily the one you think. Not just to become the next multibillionaire. Abstractly speaking, you’re a timeline within the universal span of time.

I don’t want to spend mine preventing myself from experiencing life because I’m afraid of someone’s judgment.

Embrace solitude when you feel like it. Run as fast as you can when you can. Let yourself cry when you need to.

Care less. Care less about things that are none of your business, outside your concern, outside your control. Don’t be careless, though. Life needs attention. Sometimes I think that’s the thing we need most. You lose it for a moment, and years slip out of your life like a shadow.

And don’t neglect your emotions. They’re part of you, your sensors for experiencing life. Caring less doesn’t mean feeling less.

So capture the moments. Laugh while you have the chance. Try the things you want to experience. And don’t be harsh on yourself. I’m saying this to myself as much as I’m saying it to you.
